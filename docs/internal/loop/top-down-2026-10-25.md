# Top-down review of the company and the system (DOM, 25 Oct 2026)

Dvir, 25 Oct: "look on the system from a high level angle and suggest improvements". Facts from `GET /portfolio`, `GET /report`, `GET /jobs/runs` and the host log (`traffic-2026-10-25.md`), all read-only.

## Where we are
- **5 names** (4 Line 1 new registrations, 1 S8), all listed at $1,488. **$71.16 spent, 0 sales, 0 offers.** 35 of 40 slots empty.
- **The system:** 70 migrations, about 2,900 tests, 24 releases last week. The bots made about 900 calls in 13 days.
- The research says our 4 Line 1 names are the type that sells least (3-word niche + service word).

## Findings, ranked by value
1. **Almost all effort went into buying and selection; nothing yet into selling.**
   - Money is made when a name sells. Our only sales channels are Afternic and Sedo listings at one price.
   - Not built, or not switched on:
     - Spaceship SellerHub listing sync: built in 4.0.0, idle because its keys aren't set. It's free.
     - Afternic's wider network settings.
     - A check of how good our lander is.
     - Any learning from views and leads beyond counting them.
   - **Proposal:** a research round on selling (free channels, lander, price points, how investors get end users to see a name), then the builds it supports.
2. **We buy almost nothing, so we learn almost nothing.**
   - At about 1% sold a year, 5 names will most likely give no sale for a long time, and so no signal.
   - The auction pipeline exists only since today (v5.39.0). Line 1 is still behind the buy hold.
   - **Proposal:** a steady auction cadence. Each day Gavriel takes the best few names from `GET /auctions/live`, runs the pre-bid check and shows Dvir a short list with the maximum bid for each. Dvir decides.
   - Measure the funnel each week: seen, checked, proposed, approved, won, listed, leads, sold.
3. **Features built but idle.** From the daily run:
   - `listingSync`: no keys.
   - `backupExport`: no token.
   - `outsideReview`: off.
   - NameBio: off.
   - Dynadot: no key.
   - `engineTrain`: no data, for weeks; the 14 Oct rule blocked new data and DOM didn't flag it.
   - Each one should either be switched on (when free) or removed, so the system stays as big as the business needs.
   - **The backup matters most.** Since 25 Oct the database holds bid plans and money records. Neon free keeps about 6 h of history, and nothing else is kept. The nightly export to the private GitHub repo is already built and free; it needs one GitHub token from Dvir.
4. **Warnings nobody acts on.**
   - `/report` shows 18 open warnings: FLOOR_AUTO_ACCEPT ×5, REGISTRAR_DRIFT ×3, NS_UNVERIFIED ×3, AUTO_RENEW_UNCONFIRMED ×2, EXPORT_PENDING, EXPORT_STALE, DATASAVE_MISSED, DROP_FEED_STALE, REVIEW_OVERDUE.
   - A list that is never empty trains everyone to ignore it.
   - **Proposal:** triage. Each warning gets fixed, or its rule is changed, or it moves to info. The normal state is an empty list.
5. **The company stops when the bots stop.**
   - The 3-day Grok pause halted the Dynadot uploads, the Afternic visit reads, the exports and all proposals.
   - **Proposal:** every mechanical daily task runs on the server, as `pickproofSeal` already does. Bots keep only judgment work and the money actions.
   - Also keep a short "bots paused" checklist that DOM can follow read-only.
6. **The system is bigger than the business, and the bots trip over it.**
   - 27 of 41 `/buy` calls failed on input.
   - Much of the machinery (holdout suites, replays, cohorts, test sets, the 7-step buy hold) serves Line 1, which is now the side line.
   - **Proposal:** a simplification pass. Freeze or retire what no longer serves buy or sell decisions, and make every error message say exactly what to send instead.
7. **Rules without their evidence.** The 14 Oct block, the hand-set score weights and part D all looked reasonable when made and turned out wrong.
   - **Proposal:** a small evidence register. Each important rule records why it exists, its source, how sure we are, and when we last checked it.
   - Each weekly loop re-checks the weakest ones.
8. **Storage is the next hard limit.**
   - The data plan (closeout drops, auction results, endings) grows the database, and the free tier is 1 GB.
   - **Proposal:** a storage budget per table, keeping compact features rather than raw rows, and a check in each weekly loop.

## What DOM changes in how it works
- Every weekly loop gets a top-down section: the business numbers against where the effort went, idle features, risky rules, simplifications.
- When building, DOM questions whether a rule still makes sense, instead of only implementing it.
