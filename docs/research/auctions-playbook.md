# Expired-name auctions: what experienced investors do (research, 25 Oct 2026)

Why: Dvir, 25 Oct 2026, made auctions the main effort of the S8 line ("Let's raise the limit to 250 and make the auctions our main effort") and asked us to learn from others' experience first. Public guides and articles only; no marketplace scraping, no Wayback, no NameBio automation (founder rules). Sources at the end.

## 1. How the two auctions we may use work
| | GoDaddy expired auctions | Dynadot expired auctions |
|---|---|---|
| When a name appears | about 26–29 days after it expired | after the renewal grace period |
| Length | about 10 days | 7–11 days |
| Bidding | proxy bid: you enter your maximum, the system bids the smallest step needed for you | proxy bid optional |
| Bid step | $5 between $5 and $499 | — |
| Late bids | a bid in the last 5 minutes adds 5 minutes, so "sniping" in the last second doesn't work | same, no limit on extensions |
| After the end | pay; the name moves to your account | pay within 48 h; the name moves to your Dynadot account |
| If nobody bids (GoDaddy) | a closeout at a falling fixed price (reported $50, $40, $30, $11, $5) | — |
| Membership | GoDaddy Auctions membership, about $5 a year | a funded account (at least $5) |

**Redemption risk:** at GoDaddy the old owner can still take the name back for about two to three weeks after the auction ends. Then the winner gets a refund. So a win is final only after that window.

Not allowed for us: NameJet, SnapNames, DropCatch (catch services, founder rule 5).

## 2. How experienced investors bid
1. **Value first, bid second.** Decide what the name is worth before looking at the current bid.
2. **Fixed maximum, set early.** Enter it as a proxy bid and walk away. Auction excitement ("auction psychology") is the most cited cause of losses: "$800 becomes $1,400 one step at a time".
3. **Leave room for the margin.** Experienced buyers pay about a third to a fifth of the realistic resale price (wholesale vs retail is usually 3–5x). Example from a guide: comparable sales around $3,000 → bid at most $600.
4. **Count the full cost:** winning bid + first-year renewal + fees.
5. **A lost auction costs nothing; an overpaid win costs real money.**
6. **The winner's curse:** the winner is usually the bidder who valued the name highest, so the one most likely to have overestimated it. The fixed maximum is the defence.

## 3. What sells to businesses (end users)
- `.com`, short, easy to say and spell, sounds like a real business; no hyphens, no misspellings.
- A clear buyer group: businesses that would use exactly this name.
- Signs of real demand: other endings (.net, .org, .co) taken by active businesses, ad prices for the keyword, past use.
- A trademark check before bidding: a name that matches a trademark can be lost in a dispute.

## 4. How often names sell
- 1–2% of a portfolio a year is normal; about 3% for `.com` counting sales of $100 and up.
- Names with an auction history (bids = proof of demand) sell more often than hand registrations and closeouts.
- So a portfolio of 10–15 names can easily go a year with no sale. That is normal, not failure.

## 5. What this means for us
- **Our price fits the rule of thumb.** Our Buy Now is $1,488 (about $1,200 to us after commission). A $250 maximum is about a fifth of that, in line with the 3–5x margin investors use.
- **Every auction bid needs a written maximum** before bidding, with its reason (who would buy it, other endings, comparable sales). Dvir approves the name *and* the maximum; he enters it as a proxy bid on the registrar website.
- **Wait out the redemption window** before treating a won name as ours (import it into the system only after the transfer is final).
- **Data we already have:** the daily save downloads GoDaddy's four official auction files every day, but keeps only auctions that reached $500 (CR-075). Names between $12 and $250 with bids, the range we now buy in, are thrown away. Keeping them, with bid counts and end times, would let Gavriel see each day's live auctions in our range.
