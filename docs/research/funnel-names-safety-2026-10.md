# Research: the new-registration funnel, name invention and safety checks (25 Oct 2026)

**Why:** Dvir, 25 Oct 2026: "can we investigate online what is the most quality funnel...", "bot invent names... we must make this part more accurate", "Can we do another research for the safety checks?". The research standard was his too: no single post counts as a fact, many sources, each graded for reliability.

**Method:** three researchers, about 40–60 sources each (about 130 in all). Each source is graded:
- **A:** official data, large samples or legal decisions;
- **B:** experienced practitioner with a public track record;
- **C:** general guide or forum thread;
- **D:** vendor marketing, a single anecdote or an AI-written content farm.

Conclusions below rest on A/B sources unless marked.

**Caveats:**
- Several sites quoted in DOM's first auctions research the same morning are content farms (domainnamelovers.com, domaindetails.com, seo.domains, monefy). They are graded D and are not relied on here.
- Two podcasts (Josh Reason, Phil Davis) have no transcript, so they count only through their summaries.
- One researcher read some NamePros pages through a reader proxy that gets around the site's bot protection. **That is not allowed for future research.** No conclusion here rests on those pages alone; the same points come from other sources.

## 1. The base numbers (agreed across A/B sources)
- **How often names sell:** a hand-registered portfolio sells about **0.5–1.1% of its names a year**.
  - Bob Hawkes, who analyses sales data, estimates about 0.5% for portfolios built from hand-regs, closeouts and drops, and about 1.1% for his own (NamePros, 2024–26).
  - Mike Sullivan: 97% of his hand-regs never sell.
  - Mike Mann: about 0.66%.
- **3-word names sell far less often per name than 2-word names.** One experienced investor puts 3-word names below 0.1% a year.
- **Price:** the median NamePros-reported sale is **$1,300**, and the median hold before a sale is 11 months (Hawkes, H1 2024). Buy Now sales outnumber negotiated ones about 4 to 1. **Our $1,488 Buy Now is in the right band.**
- **Where buyers come from:** most are inbound and type the **exact name** into a registrar search. About 46% of one portfolio's sales came through the Afternic registrar network, about 30% through GoDaddy search.
- **What it means for 40 names:** with 40 names held 2 years at 1% a year, the chance of **no sale at all is about 45%**; at 3% it is about 9%. One sale (about $1.2k net) pays for every renewal. **The real risk is zero sales, and 40 names can't statistically tell 1% from 3%.**

## 2. Auctions first: does it still hold?
**Yes, but for a better reason than the morning's research, and with moderate confidence.**
- **Our binding limit is the slots, not the money.** We have 40 names, a 2-year hold and 10 S8 slots. So each slot should go to the name with the best chance of selling within 24 months.
- **For:**
  - Bids are outside proof that other people value the name.
  - Brad Mugford (B) says aftermarket quality beats hand-reg quality even at low prices, and that reviewing existing names is more efficient than inventing available ones.
  - "Most available names are available for a reason" (Mugford). Allemann asks the same question: why did nobody else pay $10 for it?
- **Against:**
  - Andrew Allemann (B, July 2026) moved from expired names to hand-regs because expired-name prices rose.
  - Sullivan, Josh Reason, Phil Davis and Elliot Silver run profitable hand-reg portfolios.
  - **But** all of them run **hundreds to thousands** of cheap names. That model needs volume we don't have.
- **Verdict:** keep auctions as the main effort. Keep new registrations as a small side line under the strict rules in §3.

## 3. New registrations: the recommended funnel
**Must pass, in order:**
1. **Safety** (§5).
2. **Shape:** .com, **1–2 words**, 3 words only when the whole phrase is already common in the industry, about 15 letters or fewer, no hyphens or digits.
   - Evidence: 1 word averaged $1,242, 2 words $717, 3+ words $475 (one day of sales above $100, B/C).
   - Hawkes (A): the 3-word names that sell are common exact phrases, like a product or service, a category, geo + service, or a common saying.
3. **Present demand** (Tier A or Tier B):
   - **Tier A** (our current rule): .net and .org of the exact name both live as unrelated businesses, no trademark owner.
   - **Tier B** (new): the term is registered in **5 or more endings** **and** at least 2 live, unrelated businesses use it.
   - Evidence for the 5-ending threshold: on Squadhelp, names registered in 5+ endings sold about 3x as often as names in 1 (A, vendor data, 2021). Hawkes' $25k+ .com sales show price rising with the ending count (A).
   - **Caveat:** endings held by investors or parked pages don't count; they must be real businesses.
4. **Buyer pool:** a category with many paying small businesses: local and professional services, B2B, SaaS/tools, health, law, home, real estate, finance operations.
   - Evidence: Afternic's top-selling keywords month after month (A).
   - Funded startups mostly buy brandables: 72% of 1,000 funded companies, and only 1 in 715 used a pure category keyword (Atom, A-data/vendor). So descriptive names sell to small and service businesses, not AI startups.
5. **No future-trend terms.** A trend term needs real commercial users today plus 2-word sales already. Trend names stay a small share of slots.
   - Evidence: AI names from 2017 sold nothing for 5+ years. Crypto, meta and NFT names were mostly dropped (Allemann B; Hawkes B).

**Ranking signals (not gates):**
1. Count of live businesses using the term.
2. Endings count.
3. Sales-to-listings ratio of the keyword. Example: "agentic" had 122 sales against about 16,500 listings.
4. Siblings share.
5. Afternic search history after listing.
6. The 0–100 score.

**Changes to today's checks:**
- **Siblings ≥25%** becomes a ranking signal, not a gate. There's no outcome evidence for it, and it must count real businesses, not pages that merely resolve.
- **"Keyword appears in sales"** becomes either the sales-to-listings ratio, or "at least one similar-pattern sale ≥ $1k in the last 3 years". A human looks that up in NameBio by hand; never automated.
- **EV and renewal ratio** become sanity checks. Every .com costs the same to renew, and the break-even is about 1% a year at $1,488. The sell-through inputs are uncertain by ±100%.
- **The 0–100 score** stays a tiebreaker. Its weights were set by hand, two of its seven parts are the bots' own opinion, and one part isn't built.

**What this says about the four names we already own:**
- `aievalsconsulting.com`, `ukcbamcompliance.com`, `roofingvoiceagent.com` and `promptinjectionaudit.com` are 3-word names joining a new niche term to a service word. The evidence says this type sells least.
- Keep them: they're paid for and listed.
- At renewal (Oct 2027), drop any with no leads or Afternic searches.
- Buy no more of this type.

## 4. Inventing names: the recommended method
1. **Start from a buyer group, not a word.** Prove there are hundreds to thousands of operating firms that use the phrase (OpenCorporates, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Google Maps).
   - A niche with tens of firms fails Hawkes' funnel: 250,000 businesses → about 2 realistic buyers.
2. **Generate the exact phrase a buyer would type:** a term + a business noun (Services, Solutions, Group, Law, Pro, Labs, Health), or an industry + service pair. Never stack two niche words plus a suffix.
3. **Use AI only for breadth.** The model lists service types, synonyms and how buyers phrase their services.
   - **For each candidate it must also name 5 real businesses that would buy it, and code verifies they exist.** Unverified evidence means reject.
   - Ask for less obvious variants. Evidence: different AI models give nearly the same "creative" output (arXiv 2501.19361, A), so other investors' bots propose the same names first.
4. **Evidence gate per name: at least 3 of:**
   - (a) a company count for the term above a threshold;
   - (b) similar-pattern sales ≥ $1k in the last 3 years (looked up by hand);
   - (c) keyword ad price above $0, and Afternic searches once listed;
   - (d) the .net/.co/.io/.ai of the exact name used by an operating business;
   - (e) the term's sales-to-listings ratio.
5. **Learning loop:** at 6–12 months, use Afternic search counts and repeat visits to see which patterns get interest. With 40 names, sales alone can't measure that.

**Stop:**
- treating "available" or an AI-written buyer story as demand;
- aiming descriptive names at AI startups;
- trusting appraisal tools for unregistered names (15 appraisal tools tested, unreliable: DNW, May 2026, B);
- reading weeks without a sale as a signal.

## 5. Safety checks: the recommended checklist
**Key facts:**
- **How UDRP cases go:** in 2025 WIPO had a record 6,282 cases. About 5% were denied, about 15% settled, and the rest were transferred. More than 95% of decisions order a transfer, mostly against obvious brand abuse (WIPO, GigaLaw, A).
  - Careful investors holding descriptive names usually win. Example: 5starreview.com, bought at a GoDaddy auction, where the complaint was denied.
  - Investors lose on:
    - coined or distinctive terms a live business uses (orcid.com, and NexusMutual.com at $53k, both 2026);
    - a mark + a generic word;
    - typos.
- **Biggest gap: rights without a registered trademark.** NexusMutual.com was lost on unregistered rights. The panel said the buyer "should have at least done a Google search for the name before bidding". A trademark-register check is not enough.
- **An expired name counts as a new registration on the day we buy it** (WIPO Overview 3.0 §3.9). So the trademark check must run **at bid time**, against marks that exist then.
- **Recorded screening protects us.** Panels credit investors who screened against online databases (WIPO §3.2.3; the cnerd.com case, 2026). Store the evidence with timestamps.
- **Past use:**
  - Parked history is harmless, which matches our HIST-2 rule.
  - Google resets link credit for a new owner, but manual actions and Search Console history carry over.
  - Harmful history mainly causes buyer friction and blocklist trouble.
- **Blocklists show today only.** Spamhaus DBL listings expire once the abuse stops. A clean result doesn't prove a clean past; a listed result is a strong kill.
- **Auction traps:**
  - The old owner can redeem: at GoDaddy up to about 30 days after expiry, but not once a bid is placed; at Dynadot during the full 40-day grace period, with a refund. **Don't list a won name until RDAP shows the transfer complete.**
  - A UDRP complaint locks a name within 2 business days.
  - A stolen name can be reversed for 12 months (ICANN TDRP).

**Must pass (kill on fail):**

| # | Check | Source | Cost | Today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Live US mark: exact or core string, or a mark contained in the name | USPTO bulk XML index (no official search API; tmsearch's internal JSON is an undocumented endpoint, forbidden) | free | partly (TM-1) |
| 2 | **Exact-string web and business use:** an operating business called exactly this, on a coined name, kills it | Google exact phrase + company registries, by hand (Google's Custom Search API closes to new users, ends 1 Jan 2027) | free | **new** |
| 3 | EU mark for **every** name | EUIPO Trademark Search API | free | regulation names only |
| 4 | Brand typo / brand inside the name: edit distance 1 (≤10 letters) or 2 (longer), look-alike letters, brand contained in name, against the Tranco top list + our big-company list | Tranco (free) + dnstwist-style code | free | weaker (spelling) |
| 5 | Google Web Risk | Lookup API | free ≤100k a month | yes |
| 6 | **Spamhaus DBL** | DNS with a free DQS key; check whether our use is "commercial" under the terms first | free at low volume | **new** |
| 7 | SURBL | DNS | free under limits | yes |
| 8 | **URIBL black** (grey and red are warnings only) | DNS | free at low volume | **new** |
| 9 | Harmful history (malware, phishing, adult, pharma, gambling, spam, cloned brand site) | Wayback by hand | free | yes (HIST-2) |
| 10 | RDAP status clean; after an auction, the transfer complete before listing | RDAP | free | partly |

**Warnings for a human:**
- a dead coined US mark;
- a live mark in another class on a non-descriptive term;
- URIBL grey or red;
- zero Google pages on a name that used to be live;
- spammy backlink anchors;
- a former owner that was a real company;
- a registrar change just before expiry;
- the redemption window still open;
- an AI word or regulation acronym combined with a company or product name;
- a past UDRP decision on the exact name (WIPO and Forum search);
- WIPO Global Brand Database hits.

**Always:** store every query, hit and timestamp in the audit log. Never turn on catch-all email on a bought name; old mail would arrive.
